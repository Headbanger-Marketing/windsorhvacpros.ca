AC Repair

Does the system run but blow warm air, fail to start, or cool less effectively than before?

When an air conditioner runs without cooling adequately, the cause may involve airflow, electrical components, a refrigerant-circuit fault, or controls. Central air and ductless equipment have different layouts, so the system type and observed symptoms help define the inspection. Describe whether the unit starts, how the indoor and outdoor components behave, and whether cooling changed gradually or suddenly. A confirmed diagnosis supports a repair decision and helps distinguish it from a replacement comparison.

Assessment identifies whether the equipment is central air or ductless, checks the reported operating symptoms, and examines relevant controls, airflow, and components. A suspected refrigerant leak calls for locating its source rather than assuming that adding refrigerant resolves the issue. The findings distinguish a repairable electrical or airflow fault from a refrigerant-circuit or major-component problem that may change the repair-versus-replacement discussion.

Trace cooling loss before selecting a repair

Weak cooling can follow restricted airflow, a control fault, or a problem in the refrigerant circuit, and those causes require different work. For central air, the assessment considers the indoor equipment, outdoor unit, and their connection; a ductless system has its own indoor heads and refrigerant lines. If cooling declined gradually or ice or unusual sounds appeared, those observations can help direct inspection without presuming a particular failed part.

Treat refrigerant concerns as leak diagnosis

If inspection suggests low refrigerant, the key question is whether the circuit is leaking and where the loss occurs. Refrigerant handling and circuit repair require qualified service, not occupant intervention. A temporary change in charge does not establish the cause. Findings about the leak location, affected components, and system condition help determine whether a repair is practical and what further checks are appropriate before operation resumes.

Distinguish component repair from replacement

A repair recommendation should follow evidence about the failed component and the overall condition of the equipment. A discrete electrical or control fault may be a different decision from compressor damage or repeated refrigerant-circuit problems. Compare the proposed repair with the unit's service history, age, and compatibility with the existing system. Replacement becomes a separate option when condition or repair scope makes continued service uncertain, not simply because cooling has weakened.

Equipment observations to record

  • Indoor and outdoor model information
  • Rooms affected and temperature pattern
  • Ice, leakage, noise, or displayed fault
  • Recent filter changes or earlier repairs

Keep the assessment safe

Do not open electrical panels or handle refrigerant. If ice, water near electrical equipment, or abnormal operation is present, stop using the affected system and request an assessment.
